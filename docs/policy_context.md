# Policy context

This note sets out the policy context for the findings: building control, tree planting, heat planning and heat research in Lagos. It uses only facts from sources that could be opened. Each fact is cited with its URL and access date. Facts are attributed to the source that reports them and are not checked further.

## Building control and violations

Nairametrics, "Lagos records over 27,000 building violations in three years", Caleb Obiowo, 22 May 2026. https://nairametrics.com/2026/05/22/lagos-records-over-27000-building-violations-in-three-years/ (accessed 2026-11-03).

The article reports figures disclosed at the Lagos State Government's 2026 Annual Ministerial Press Briefing:

- 27,233 building violations were recorded between May 2023 and April 2026. They were identified through enforcement operations of the Lagos State Building Control Agency (LASBCA) and involved developers and property owners who breached physical planning regulations.
- 4,711 developers obtained "Authorisation to Commence Construction" for approved projects in the same period.
- The state has set a target of up to 45,000 building permits a year. According to official data from a May 2025 ministerial briefing, it issued 37,266 building planning permits over the previous six years.

The article gives no breakdown of the violations by LGA or by type, and it does not mention setbacks or open space.

## Tree planting

AllAfrica, republishing Premium Times: "Nigeria: Sanwo-Olu Urges Lagos Residents to Plant Trees, Says State Targets 20,000 This Year", Emmanuel Agbo, 14 July 2026. https://allafrica.com/stories/202607150132.html (accessed 2026-11-03).

The article reports statements made at the 2026 Tree Planting Day, according to a statement from the Lagos State Ministry of Environment and Water Resources:

- The state aims to plant at least 20,000 trees in 2026. By 30 June, 13,401 trees, about 67% of the target, had been planted across the five divisions of Lagos, and planting was to continue until October.
- A tree-planting campaign covers the 57 Local Government Areas and Local Council Development Areas. Their chairpersons are designated "Tree Custodians", expected to nurture and monitor newly planted trees for at least 12 months.
- Under Lagos State law, anyone who unlawfully fells a tree must replace it with five others.
- Free seedlings are available from the nurseries of the Lagos State Parks and Gardens Agency (LASPARK) in Ikorodu and Agege.

## Heat risk assessment and heat action plan

C40 Cities Climate Leadership Group, "Request for Proposal (RfP): Lagos State Urban Heat Assessment", July 2026. https://www.c40.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/RfP_Lagos-Heat-Risk-Assessment.pdf (accessed 2026-11-03; text extracted from the PDF).

The request is issued under C40's Resilient Future Cities Program and states the following:

- A consultant is sought to carry out an Urban Heat Assessment for Lagos State, covering the urban heat island effect, heat waves, extreme temperatures and other heat-related hazards. The assessment feeds into the Lagos Climate Change Risk Assessment and the Lagos Climate Action Plan (CAP 2026).
- The assessment "needs to establish the baseline heat evidence required to develop an Urban Heat Action Plan planned by the city in 2027" (p. 3). Its findings "will inform a city-wide heat action plan, being scheduled for 2027", with actions over "a 5 – 10 years' timeline".
- According to the document, "no dedicated heat management and action plan exists for Lagos", and extreme heat "has never been assessed as a standalone hazard" (p. 3).
- The 2021 Climate Change Risk Assessment identified Lagos Island, Lagos Mainland, Agege, Kosofe and Oshodi-Isolo as the LGAs most severely affected by heatwaves and the urban heat island effect. The new assessment is asked to work at LGA level, and ideally at ward or community level, across all 20 LGAs (p. 3).
- Land-use change, densification and loss of green cover are named as drivers of the expansion of the urban heat island (pp. 3–4).
- Maintaining the assessment is to involve coordination between the Ministry of the Environment and Water Resources and the Ministry of Health.
- Final report submission and project completion are set for 29 January 2027.

## Heat and health research

ThisDay, "Cambridge, UNILAG Identify 10 Locations for Urban Heat Research in Lagos", Esther Oluku, 23 July 2026 (date taken from the URL). https://www.thisdaylive.com/2026/07/23/cambridge-unilag-identify-10-locations-for-urban-heat-research-in-lagos/ (accessed 2026-11-03).

The article reports on the Hotsteppers Project, which is funded by the Wellcome Trust:

- The University of Cambridge and the University of Lagos lead a consortium that includes the Lagos State Government, the Lagos Urban Development Initiative (LUDI) and the Lagos Metropolitan Area Transportation Authority (LAMATA).
- The project is a three-year study of the health risks of extreme heat across 10 selected settlements in Lagos. It aims to co-design infrastructure and lifestyle interventions.
- The article does not name the 10 settlements.
- A co-principal investigator from the University of Lagos is quoted as saying that most climate adaptation plans and policies of the government "did not embed heat".
